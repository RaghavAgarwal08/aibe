    What to expect

    Your first ninety minutes.

    Bring a laptop if you have one. If you don't, come anyway and pair with someone.

    1. First 10 minutes

      Announcements

      What's coming up, which project teams are recruiting, who's speaking this month.

    2. Next 60

      The workshop

      One skill, taught by an officer, a member or a guest. You follow along on your own idea — or on a sample one we provide if you don't have one yet.

      Officers stick around. If you want to talk through an idea, this is the easiest time to do it.

    Membership

    Two levels. You choose which one you want.

    Level one

    General Member

    Register with AIBE and attend at least one event. That's it.

    • Free admission to all events, workshops and demo days
    • Eligible to join or form a project team
    • Access to mentors and introductions to campus entrepreneurship resources

    Level two

    Active Member

    Attend ten general body meetings or workshops in a semester — or simply join a project team.

    • Everything a General Member gets
    • Eligible to lead a project team
    • Eligible to apply for officer roles
    • Can propose constitutional amendments in writing to the board

    If you're an Active Member and not on a team next semester, a short continuation form from the Membership Director keeps your status. On a team, it carries over automatically.

    Membership is open to all UIUC students in good standing, without regard to race, religion, creed, color, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, national origin, age, disability, veteran status, or any other status protected by University policy or law.

    Leadership

    How you become an officer.

    AIBE doesn't hold general elections. Officers are selected by the Executive Board through an open application, and the process is the same for everyone.

    Meet the current board
    1. Step 1 · eligibility

      Be an Active Member

      A UIUC student in good standing, an Active Member for at least one semester, and willing to commit to a one-year term.

    2. Step 2 · the open call

      Applications open

      In the last four weeks of spring semester, every Active Member gets a public call at least seven business days before applications close. Anyone eligible may apply, and anyone may recommend someone else.

    3. Step 3 · apply

      A résumé and a plan

      A short application: your résumé and what you'd actually do with the role.

    4. Step 4 · interview

      Everyone eligible is interviewed

      The board interviews every applicant who meets the requirements, against the same published criteria.

    5. Step 5 · decision

      Board vote, written notice

      The board selects by majority vote; an officer never votes on a position they're seeking. Everyone is notified in writing and the new roster goes out to the membership.

    Questions

    Things people ask.

    Do I need to know how to code?

    No. A good share of what we teach — customer discovery, pricing, marketing, analytics, operations — has nothing to do with writing code, and the parts that do are taught with tools that don't require you to. Teams here work best when they're mixed.

    Do I need a business background?

    Also no. If you can build, you are extremely useful here on day one, and the business side is exactly what we'll teach you.

    Do I need to have an idea already?

    No. Each semester the board publishes a Project Slate of defined projects and challenge prompts. Join one of those, or get matched into a team. Bring your own idea only if you want to.

    What does it cost?

    Dues are $5 per member per semester. The Treasurer can waive them for anyone with financial need, and nobody is excluded from a general body meeting for being unable to pay. Field trips are the one extra — roughly $40–50 toward transport, and entirely optional.

    How much time does it take?

    A general member can come to a weekly workshop and nothing else — about ninety minutes. If you join a project team, expect a few hours a week on top, and more in the run-up to demo day. The hackathons are a full Saturday each.

    What's the difference between a General and an Active Member?

    You become a General Member by registering with AIBE and coming to one event. You become an Active Member by attending ten general body meetings or workshops in a semester, or by joining a project team. Active Members can lead project teams and apply for officer roles.

    Can I join part-way through the semester?

    Yes. Workshops stand alone, and teams can take new people. Later in the semester it's harder to join a team that's already deep into a build, but it still happens.

    Who owns what I build?

    You do. Ideas, work product and any venture that grows out of a project team belong to the people who created them. AIBE takes no ownership, equity, revenue share or any other financial interest. If your team plans to keep going after the semester, we'll push you hard to write a founder agreement early.

    Can my venture use the AIBE name?

    You can say your project started in AIBE. You can't represent yourself as acting on behalf of AIBE or the University without written approval from the Executive Board.

    Are freshmen welcome?

    Very. Starting something in your first year and having it not work is close to a free option.

    Are graduate students welcome?

    Membership is open to all UIUC students in good standing, undergraduate and graduate.

    Can I leave?

    Any time, without fear of retribution, retaliation or harassment. That's in the constitution too.
