    Why we exist

    Illinois has AI clubs. Illinois has startup clubs. This is the room where the two meet.

    AI is changing how companies get started faster than any syllabus can keep up with. A student with the right tools can now do, in a weekend, what used to take a seed round and a team of five.

    Most AI organisations on campus point at the research. Most entrepreneurship organisations teach the business and leave the building to someone else. AIBE sits in the gap: we treat AI as the thing that lets one student go from a half-formed idea to a working product, a real customer conversation, and a pitch — inside a single semester.

    You bring the idea. We bring the workshops, the teammates, the mentors, and the deadline.

    • Hands-on sessions where you leave with an artifact, not notes
    • Projects you choose — software, services, media, physical products
    • Ownership stays with you: AIBE takes no equity and no revenue share

    What a member actually does

    Four things, every semester.

    Weekly

    Workshops that make something

    Ninety minutes, laptops open. Market research with AI, automating a workflow, shipping a landing page, reading your own unit economics. You leave each one with a file, a flow, or a page that didn't exist before.

    All semester

    A project team of your own

    Pick something off the semester's Project Slate, or bring your own idea and recruit two or three people who want to build it. Milestones along the way, a demo day at the end.

    Twice a semester

    Full-day hackathons

    One day, food, API credits and compute provided. Use everything the workshops taught you to get from blank page to MVP and business model. The winning team gets funding and mentorship to keep going.

    Monthly

    Time with people who've done it

    Fireside chats with alumni founders, panels with PMs and analysts using AI daily, investors on what they actually fund, and faculty from Gies and Siebel working on this for a living.

    The arc

    How a semester runs.

    Everything is built around one deadline: demo day. The workshops feed the projects, the projects feed the demos, and the hackathons are where teams find each other.

    See the full programme
    1. Weeks 1–2

      Kickoff & info session

      What AIBE is, what's on the Project Slate this semester, and a social so you meet people before you have to pick a team.

    2. Weeks 2–14 · weekly

      Hands-on workshops

      The core of the club. A new skill every week, taught so that someone with no CS background and someone with no business background both leave able to use it.

    3. Weeks 3–5

      Teams form around the Slate

      Join a posted project, get matched into one, or pitch your own and recruit. Minimum two people, named team lead, registered with the Secretary.

    4. Mid-semester

      Hackathon #1

      A full day: food, API credits, GPU compute. Blank page to MVP and business model between breakfast and the evening pitch.

    5. Throughout · monthly

      Firesides, panels & socials

      Founders, operators, investors, faculty. Plus the un-programmed part: co-founder interviews, startup showcases, and the occasional field trip.

    6. Finals weeks

      Demo day

      Every project team shows what it built to a room of judges, mentors and members. Hackathon #2 and the semester's funding decision land here.

    Who this is for

    Teams work because the people in them don't match.

    The constitution says it plainly: AIBE exists to put business, engineering, CS, the sciences, the humanities and the professional schools in the same room. Here's what that looks like in practice.

    If you can build

    You've never had to find a customer

    You can ship. What you haven't done is figure out who it's for, what they'd pay, or whether the thing is worth building at all. That's most of what we teach.

    If you can sell

    You've been waiting on a technical co-founder

    In 2026 you don't have to. You'll learn enough to get a prototype in front of real people yourself — and you'll be the most useful person on any team here.

    If you're neither yet

    You just want to try something

    No experience required, and that's not a courtesy line. Come to one workshop, join one team, see whether you like the feeling. Plenty of people find out they do.

    Get in

    There is no application. Just show up.

    Join the Discord to see what's happening this week, fill out the interest form so we know you're coming, and turn up to a meeting. That's the whole process.
